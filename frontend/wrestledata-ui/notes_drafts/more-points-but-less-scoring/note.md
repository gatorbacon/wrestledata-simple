The results are in and it's not a great story for the 3-point takedown. 

In fact, it's a terrible one.  The data supports some of the more cynical predictions when 3-point takedowns were proposed, so I suspect we'll hear some I-told-you-so's in the comments.

I analyzed every point scored in the last 6 NCAA National Tournaments, which totals 3,840 matches, between the best D1 wrestlers in the country.  That's 1,920 matches in the 2-point takedown era, and 1,920 matches in the 3-point takedown era.  

Here's what I found...

While there was an increase in the overall number of points scored, there was a decrease in the number of actual scoring events.  

But it gets worse.  

Because it's not just that scoring events went down, they actually decreased in **every** category for **both** wrestlers, the winners and the losers.  Fewer takedowns, fewer escapes, fewer reversals, fewer nearfalls.  We even had fewer riding time points. 

It gets worse still...

I lied above when I said scoring went down in every category.  There **is** one category where scoring did go up for the 3pt takedown era.  Want to guess what it is?

Stalling penalties.

There was an 84% increase in stall points awarded to the losing wrestler (meaning the winning wrestler was stalling).  Which is the logical outcome of a 4-2 lead, instead of the old 3-2 lead, because now you have a point to give.

To break it down:
* In the 2-pt era, 35% of winners gave up a stall warning, but only 6% gave up a point.  
* In the 3-pt era, 42% of winners gave up a stall warning, and 11.6% gave up a point.  

And this stalling correlates well with the 24% drop in takedowns by the losing wrestler. Because its difficult to takedown a guy who is stalling, and now that winning wrestler has a point to give because he's up 4-2 in the third, instead of 3-2.
